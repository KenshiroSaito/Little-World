# src/world: the tree renderer (Member A)

## For the main README: rendering and the camera

- **One canvas, two layers of work.** The world is painted into 2D canvases in
  world pixels (16 px tiles): terrain once, the tree whenever growth changes,
  a one-pixel-per-tile darkness map, and an overlay for relics and highlights.
  A three.js stage shows them as nearest-filtered planes, so pixel art stays
  crisp, and adds the effects. Without WebGL the same layers are drawn in 2D.
- **Procedural art from the guide.** B's `buildTree` decides where every
  branch and root goes. The pixels come from the module kit cut out of
  `worldtreeprocedural.jpeg`: bark is texture-mapped along each segment, root
  flares grow with the number of roots, leaf clusters are assembled from the
  kit's base-green clusters and recoloured to the activity's palette, and each
  leaf colour gets its branch type (oak, willow, pine, fruiting).
- **Effects tied to play.** Only glowing things bloom (roots, new growth,
  relics, the lit ruin's inscriptions, the spirit). A record sends a shockwave
  and a burst of particles in its colour from the new branch; reaching the ruin
  flashes it with god rays. Fireflies match each branch's leaf colour, so the
  variety of care is visible. Reduced motion turns off the growing-in,
  shockwaves, bursts, camera easing and idle animation.
- **Camera.** Zoom snaps to whole-pixel scales from 1x to 6x (device pixels per
  world pixel are always whole). Wheel and pinch zoom around the pointer; drag
  pans; the view is clamped to the world. Fit tree frames everything from the
  canopy to the deepest root. After a record the camera eases to the new growth.
- **The spirit.** The tree spirit from `maincharacter.jpeg` sits in the canopy,
  just off the highest leaves, idling and smiling after each record. C's
  SpiritBubble shows what it says. Arrow keys pan the camera.

## Contract with B and C

- `<TreeCanvas onSelect />` keeps the props of main's placeholder, so C's
  `App.tsx` works unchanged; everything else is optional.
- In: `segments` (from `buildTree`; the bare sapling if left out),
  `revealedRelics` ids, `inscriptionCount`, `barkColor` (one colour for all the
  wood), `paintMode`, `reducedMotion` and `spiritEnabled`. Segment coordinates
  are tiles, x right of the trunk and y below the surface (see `types.ts`).
- Layer depths assume the taproot grows 2 tiles per record: 20 LP reaches
  `STONE_TOP` (row 8) and 40 LP reaches the ruin (row 16). See `layout.ts`.
- Out: `onSelect(selection)` with a `CanvasSelection` from `types.ts`
  (`segment` with an id, `relic` with an id, or `tablet`) for clicks.

## Files

- `renderer.ts` input, camera, layers; `stage.ts` and
  `shaders.ts` the three.js side; `particles.ts` GPU particles.
- `terrain.ts`, `tree.ts`, `treeKit.ts`, `lighting.ts`, `overlay.ts` paint the layers.
- `camera.ts`, `treeShape.ts`, `hitTest.ts`, `spirit.ts` are pure and tested in `world.test.ts`.
- `fixtures.ts` is the hand-made tree used until `buildTree` lands.
- `artLayout.ts` and `assets/*.png` are generated by `tools/extract_art.py`.
